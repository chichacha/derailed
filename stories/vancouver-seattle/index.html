How did Vancouver move one hour away from Seattle?

The border didn’t move. The distance didn’t change. But on November 1, 2026, the clocks on either side stopped agreeing.

Until recently, winter worked the same way in Vancouver and Seattle: both fell back together. In 2026, British Columbia decided that most of the province would stay at UTC−7 year-round. Washington would continue to change clocks.

Try it: Start with Seattle. Switch from October 31 to November 2. Then switch the destination to San Francisco. The geography is unchanged; the time difference is not.

An international border became a clock boundary.

On October 31, Vancouver, Seattle and San Francisco all observe UTC−7. After the change in the United States on November 1, Seattle and San Francisco observe UTC−8. Vancouver stays at UTC−7.

When Vancouver says 9:00 AM on November 2, Seattle and San Francisco say 8:00 AM.

The effect lasts through the US winter. When the US springs forward again in March 2027, the West Coast cities share the same clock once more—unless the rules change again.

British Columbia wasn’t uniform before 2026.

Vancouver and Victoria formerly moved their clocks twice a year. Fort St. John, by contrast, already observed UTC−7 year-round. Some communities in southeastern BC have followed other Mountain Time arrangements. The 2026 change affects the relationships between those clocks; it doesn’t erase their histories.

A place name doesn’t determine the clock rule. Neither does its province, by itself.

One place. Three different pieces of data.

Location: GeoNames supplies representative points for the cities shown above.

Time-zone identity: GeoNames also supplies IANA names like America/Vancouver and America/Los_Angeles. These are not numerical UTC offsets.

Applicable clock rule: Government decisions and time-zone software determine which offset applies at a particular date. For this demonstration, we have explicitly recorded and sourced the offsets for two UTC instants so that older computers cannot silently rewrite BC’s new rules.

This is why the map and the clocks are built from separate data. Turf.js calculates geographical distance; it does not calculate time zones.

Nothing moved. Except the relationship.

A four-hour journey across a border is still four hours. But the local departure and arrival clocks can now suggest something different.
