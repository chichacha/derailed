How did Vancouver move one hour away from Seattle?

Nothing moved on the map. But on November 1, 2026, the clocks on either side of the border stopped agreeing.

The two cities are as close as they were yesterday. British Columbia decided to keep most of its clocks on UTC−7, while Washington State still turns back to UTC−8 for winter. The result? Vancouver and Seattle no longer share the same winter time.

02 / Same coastline, different winter

On October 31, Vancouver, Seattle and San Francisco are all at UTC−7. On November 2, Washington and California have fallen back to UTC−8 while Vancouver stays on UTC−7.

At 9:00 AM in Vancouver, the clock in Seattle reads 8:00 AM. The difference will disappear again when the U.S. starts daylight saving time in March 2027, assuming the rules stay the same.

And the travel distance? Exactly unchanged. The Washington State Department of Transportation notes that an approximately four-hour Amtrak journey can appear as three hours in one direction and five in the other when shown in local departure and arrival times.

03 / British Columbia never had just one clock rule

Before 2026, Vancouver and Victoria moved their clocks twice a year. Fort St. John already stayed at UTC−7 all year. Southeastern BC communities historically followed Mountain Time arrangements.

Calgary is another twist: Alberta is also staying on UTC−6 year-round from November 2026. That keeps Calgary one hour ahead of Vancouver while Seattle moves one hour behind.

One provincial boundary never guaranteed one clock rule. And a place’s IANA time-zone ID does not, by itself, explain which local government made that rule.

04 / One continent, several ideas of time

Toronto still falls back, so its winter difference from Vancouver shrinks from three hours to two. And St. John’s, Newfoundland, follows a half-hour time zone: its winter UTC offset is −3:30 rather than a whole number of hours.

The map expands, but the governing idea remains small: a geographic distance is not a time difference.

05 / How the data works

A point. GeoNames provides representative city coordinates and identifiers.

A name. GeoNames also supplies IANA identifiers such as America/Vancouver, America/Edmonton, and America/St_Johns. They encode time-zone histories, not a single permanent offset.

A rule on a date. Government decisions determine local time policies. The R + DuckDB preparation script joins the GeoNames places to explicitly documented UTC offsets for two instants and exports a small JSON file. JavaScript reads that file to update the clocks; Turf.js computes the distances.

This is deliberately a dated editorial snapshot, not a universal time-zone conversion service. The source definitions and the rules are kept separate.
