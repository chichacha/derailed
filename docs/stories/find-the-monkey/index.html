01 / THE BRIEF

A monkey.
Three balls.
One pedestal.

Imagine someone gives you a wonderfully strange assignment: teach a monkey to juggle while standing on a tall pedestal.

THE AMBITION ✳A juggling monkey, high above the ground.

There are two big jobs: build the pedestal and find out whether the monkey can learn to juggle.

Which one would you begin with?

03 / THE TRAP

A beautiful
pedestal.
Now what?

Building the pedestal feels productive because you already know how. But a finished pedestal doesn't tell you whether the monkey can juggle.

PEDESTAL WORK

What you made

Plans, paint, a polished platform.

MONKEY WORK

What you learned

Whether the risky part is even possible.

Visible progress is not always meaningful progress.

04 / THE IDEA

Find the
monkey.

The monkey is not simply the hardest task. It's the important uncertainty that could make the rest of your work irrelevant.

THE RULE ✳Find the assumption that matters most. Test it before polishing the easy parts.

You may discover that the idea is possible. You may discover it needs to change. Either way, you've learned something useful.

05 / A SMALLER AMBITION

What about
a garden?

Imagine planting a whole raised bed of vegetables. You're already picturing the harvest. Then the tiny sprouts start disappearing.

Maybe slugs ate them. Maybe the conditions weren't right. Maybe the garden didn't get enough attention. You don't know yet.

THE PEDESTAL?

Buy more seeds. Add more boxes. Plan a bigger harvest.

THE MONKEY?

Can a small number of seedlings survive—and what is stopping them?

A true story can start with a guess. A good experiment keeps the guess separate from the evidence.

06 / YOUR NEXT MOVE

The seedlings
vanished.

What would teach you the most before planting the whole box again?

YOUR MOVE

Choose an approach. None guarantees a harvest.

A cover can also change temperature and moisture. Six plants won't give a definitive verdict—but observation beats an untested story.

07 / THE PRACTICE

Big ambition.
Small experiment.

This isn't an argument against building pedestals. Eventually, you'll need one. The trick is to avoid spending all your time there before the risky idea has been tested.
