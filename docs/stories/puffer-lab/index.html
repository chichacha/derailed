Puffer Lab.

Part fish. Part porcupine. Entirely surprised.

It started with a photograph of a wonderfully boxy, big-eyed fish at Aquamarine Fukushima. Now I want to see how much of its personality can fit inside a few SVG shapes.

01 / THE ORIGINAL DETOUR

Who put a box
in the ocean?

My aquarium photo has an enormous eye, an open little mouth, pale yellow fins and spectacularly irregular dark patches. Its outline isn't quite a sphere or a rectangle. It's a fish with character.

In English, ハリセンボン is a porcupinefish. Porcupinefish and true pufferfish are close relatives, but belong to different families. Their spines and ability to inflate are defensive adaptations—not a party trick we should try on real animals.

THE DESIGN BRIEF ✳ Keep the eye. Keep the boxiness. Keep the attitude.

Abstraction is not the same as copying: we'll turn an observation into a set of drawing rules.

The original aquarium photo is the reference for the illustration. See the project notebook for the source-asset handoff.

02 / SILHOUETTE

First, draw
the box.

A fish doesn't have to be perfectly realistic to be recognizable. A broad middle, tapering face and gently squared-off rear give us the starting silhouette.

The trick: the body is one SVG path. Adjust its control points, and the silhouette changes without redrawing the entire animal.

03 / EXPRESSION

Make it
look.

One oversized, glossy eye gives the fish most of its expression. A second circle for the iris, another for the pupil and one tiny highlight are enough to make it look alert.

What's the difference between a faithful observation and a caricature? Sometimes just a few pixels of eye radius.

DRAWING RULE 02Proportions carry personality.
04 / VARIATION

Same fish.
Different spots.

Its patches and dots are not evenly spaced. That's an invitation to experiment with bounded randomness: new patterns, but only in places that make sense for the drawing.

If we give the randomizer a seed, we can recreate the same markings later. Random doesn't have to mean unrepeatable.

06 / WHAT DID WE LEARN?

A surprising amount
of fish from very little.

The hardest part wasn't drawing a hundred spots. It was finding a small enough set of rules that could draw one convincing fish in the first place.

That's the little experiment: observe something real, decide what matters, encode the decisions—and see what happens when you turn the knobs.

Field note: inspired by my own aquarium photograph at Aquamarine Fukushima. The SVG is playful illustration, not a species-identification guide. Further reading: Australian Museum — Porcupinefishes · DERAILED #25.
