FIELD GUIDE № 001 · THE CIRCLE PROBLEM

Five ways to make a circle.

Crêpe. Pancake. Tortilla. Flatbread. Galette. They can look like cousins on a plate. But their real differences begin before they hit the pan.

Explore the family ↘

A tiny interactive explainer. No frameworks. No crumbs.

01 / THE BIG REVEAL

It's not a neat family tree.

The names aren't all at the same level. Flatbread describes a broad family of breads. Tortilla is one member. Crêpe is a kind of thin pancake in the broad sense. Galette sometimes names a buckwheat cousin of the crêpe—and sometimes something entirely different.

START WITH THE MIXTURE

🥣 Pourable batter

You pour or ladle it onto a hot surface, then spread it or let it settle. The batter sets as it cooks.

Crêpe Very thin; usually no raising agent
Pancake Often thick and raised in North America
Breton galette Usually buckwheat; thin, often savoury
OR START WITH

🤲 Shapeable dough

You press, pat, stretch, or roll it before cooking. Typical flatbreads belong here.

Flatbread A whole category, not one single recipe
↳ Tortilla Corn masa or wheat flour; pressed or rolled
↳ Roti · naan · pita… Different traditions; some use yeast
The useful rule: Ask “Can I pour this mixture, or must I shape it?” before asking whether the finished food is round. This is a cooking shortcut, not a rigid scientific taxonomy.

* “Pancake” means different things around the world. British pancakes can be thin; crêpes are also pancakes in the broad culinary sense. “Galette” can also mean a free-form tart or other French baked goods.

05 / THE COUNTERTOP TEST

The no-sugar surprise.

No sugar, but still a crêpe. Why?

Because sweetness isn't the defining feature. A classic crêpe can be sweet or savoury. What matters more is a fluid batter spread into a thin sheet—not a ball of dough pressed or rolled flat.

Field sample: A Proctor Silex crêpe made with flour, milk, eggs, butter and salt. No sugar, no raising agent, spread very thin. Classification: crêpe. Verdict: delicious-looking evidence. 🥞
